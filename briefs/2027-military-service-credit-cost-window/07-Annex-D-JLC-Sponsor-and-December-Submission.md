## 1. The one-line read

**The obvious sponsor is the wrong sponsor.** The JLC member organisation with the most
Virginia legislative capability is MOAA's Virginia Council of Chapters — and a large
share of its membership is **statutorily barred from this benefit**, because
§ 51.1-142.2 B 3 blocks purchase of service already counted in a military retirement
allowance and the 10 U.S.C. ch. 1223 exception rescues **Reserve and Guard** retirees
only, not regular 20-year retirees. The organisations whose members the bill
affirmatively protects are the **Guard, Reserve and enlisted/short-service**
associations. **The recommended sponsor of record is the Virginia National Guard
Association**, which also has the best documented record of any JLC member of
converting its own adopted priorities into JLC initiatives. AUSA is the *route*
(the Legislative Officer's organisation), not the sponsor. MOAA VCOC is the
*capability partner*, not the face.

**The mechanic to know about requirement 2.** Under § 2.2-2681 a qualifying
organisation's **own president appoints** its JLC representative. The sponsorship
decision is therefore an *organisational* decision taken by the organisation's
leadership, not a decision its JLC representative can make alone — and for the
organisations that work by resolution (the Legion, VFW, VNGA), the formal instrument
is a resolution adopted at a meeting. **Inside 53 days, an organisation with a
standing resolutions process and a meeting already on its calendar is worth more than
a larger organisation without one.** Confidence **High** on the appointment mechanic
(statute); **Medium** on the resolution-timing constraint (inferred — pattern from
published resolutions pages).

## 4. The eligibility fact that decides the sponsor

This is the most consequential thing in this annex and it is not in Annex C.

**§ 51.1-142.2 B 3 bars** purchase of service credit that is "included in the
calculation of any retirement allowance received or to be received from this or
another retirement system, **except as otherwise required by Chapter 1223 of Title 10
of the United States Code**." Chapter 1223 is **Reserve Component** retirement.

Counsel and the Red Team have both construed this and agree:

- Annex A § 4 — the ch. 1223 exception "is what preserves the ability to purchase
  Reserve and National Guard service that also counts toward a military pension."
- Red Team **S-4** — "The 10 U.S.C. ch. 1223 exception preserves **Reserve/Guard**
  retirement only — it does not reach regular retirees… A regular 20-year military
  retiree's active duty is included in the allowance they already receive, so they
  cannot buy."

**Therefore the bill has three cohorts, not one:**

| Cohort | Eligibility under B 3 | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-service active-duty veterans** — 180+ consecutive days, no military retirement | **Eligible**, subject to the unresolved second limb (below) | **High** (statute, verified verbatim by Red Team) |
| **Guard and Reserve members and retirees** with 180+ consecutive days of qualifying full-time service | **Eligible, and affirmatively protected by statute** — the only cohort whose eligibility survives a military retirement allowance | **High** |
| **Regular (20-year active-duty) military retirees** | **Barred** by the first limb | **High** |

**And one open limb that cuts only against the first cohort.** B 3 also bars purchase
"if there is a balance in a defined contribution account that serves as a primary
retirement account related to such service." Red Team S-4 flags that **Blended
Retirement System** service — mandatory for entrants on or after 2018-01-01 — carries
government TSP contributions attributable to that service, and that if VRS administers
the second limb against BRS service, the youngest veterans lose access. **How VRS
administers that limb is unknown and is item 9/10 of the VRS data request.** The
Guard/Reserve cohort is the one cohort with no comparable exposure, because its
protection is express in the statute rather than dependent on VRS practice.

**Two consequences for sponsor selection.**

1. **The career-officer-retiree organisations are the worst-served members of the
   Council, not the best-served.** A bill whose public face is an organisation of
   20-year retirees invites the one question the package answers badly: *does this
   help your own members?* For a large share of MOAA's Virginia membership, the honest
   answer is no. Confidence **High** on the statutory bar; **Medium** on the
   membership-composition inference — no VCOC breakdown by retirement status was
   located, and this is reasoning from who MOAA is chartered to represent.
2. **Guard and Reserve service and Virginia public employment structurally overlap.**
   A Guard member holds a civilian job by definition, and state, local and school
   employers are among the largest employers in most Virginia localities. The overlap
   between "VNGA member" and "VRS member" is therefore structurally high in a way the
   overlap between "retired colonel" and "VRS member" is not. Confidence **Medium**
   (inferred — interest; **no Virginia-specific data on Guard members' civilian
   employers was found**, and I am not treating this as documented).

**Flag to the Drafting Specialist and to the Editor.** If VNGA is the sponsor, the
position paper's "how many people" paragraph has to be rewritten, because the current
one leads with Plan 1 members and says nothing about which cohorts B 3 excludes. Red
Team S-4 already requires that rewrite for accuracy; § 5's recommendation makes it
also a political necessity.

## 8. The 2028 fallback, stated fairly

Annex C named the slip as "a full year's delay… named rather than discovered." Naming
it fairly means naming its advantages, which are real.

| | Seek the 2027 slate on 2026-12-17 | Accept the slip to 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| **Calendar** | 53 days to the gate; 27 days from gate to convening. | Spring 2027 call → submit **NLT 2027-06-15** → summer sponsor presentation and Council vote → Aug–Oct position paper and DVS technical review, member feedback NLT 2027-09-15 → fall 2027 adopt and prioritise → Dec 2027 patron list. |
| **Session** | **2027 is the short session** — 30 days, conventionally 45. Annex C § 4.3: in a short session the tabling motion gets easier, because tabling is the cheapest way to clear a docket. | **2028 is the long session** — roughly 60 days. More hearing time per bill in the one subcommittee that decides this. |
| **Package readiness** | **Three substantive items open**: F-1's drafting choice (Annex A Q3a/Q12), the VRS data request (Q1–Q5), and Red Team S-4's two eligibility limbs. The § 30-19.1:7 statement arrives within days of prefiling and cannot be reacted to. | All three closeable first. A bill that has VRS's own numbers in hand before prefiling is a materially different bill in front of this subcommittee. |
| **Sponsor** | No sponsoring organisation exists; one must be found and must act inside 53 days. | Time for a Legion or VFW Department resolution — a durable asset the package does not have. |
| **Cost of waiting** | — | One more cohort of Plan 1 veterans retires without the option. All 110,474 active Plan 1 members are already past the deadline as a matter of law. |

**My read, offered as a read and not a decision.** On the political analysis alone the
**2028 cycle is the stronger path**, because the thing that kills this bill is an
unbounded fiscal note in a short session, and every item that would bound it is
currently open. The argument for December is not readiness — it is that a one-year
slip is a real cost to a real cohort, and that is a judgment for JLCVSO and the board,
not for me. **A third option exists and is cheap:** make the two U-1/U-8 checks in § 9
first — they are minutes of work for a person with a browser — and decide after,
since if the December add-on item is gone the decision makes itself.
