## 3. The cost model, shown in full

### 3.1 Governing identity

From F-26, actuarial equivalent cost is the plan's break-even price. Therefore:

```
Cost of the bill, per year of credit purchased
  = Actuarial Equivalent Cost  −  Approximate Normal Cost actually paid
```

If the gap is zero, the bill is free. If the gap is negative, the member subsidizes the plan.

### 3.2 Estimating AEC as a percentage of current salary

```
AEC%  =  M × G × ä × v

  M  = plan multiplier per year of service   (F-08, F-12)
  G  = salary growth factor from purchase to AFC measurement
       = (1 + A-04) ^ (years to AFC midpoint)
  ä  = COLA-adjusted life annuity factor at retirement   (A-07)
  v  = discount from retirement back to purchase date
       = 1 / (1 + A-01) ^ (years to retirement)
```

**The pattern that matters politically:** the gap is **negative or near zero for the entire Hybrid population below about age 50** — and the Hybrid plan covers every veteran hired since January 1, 2014 (F-09). The expensive cases sit in the closed Plan 1 and Plan 2 cohorts and at older ages. The 12-month pre-retirement deadline and the graded rate authority in Annex A, § 2.2 — graded on age **and** creditable service as of Annex A v1.1 — are aimed precisely at the bottom rows of these tables.

### 3.4 Total cost

**One-time catch-up:**

```
Eligible pool      = 3,400 (A-08) × 85% (A-09) × 90% (A-10)  ≈  2,600
Purchasers         = 2,600 × 5%–15% (A-11)                   =  130 – 390
Years of credit    = purchasers × 2.5 (A-12)                 =  325 – 975
One-time cost      = years × $1,500 (A-13)                   =  $488,000 – $1,463,000
Over 5 years (A-15)                                          ≈  $98,000 – $293,000 / yr
```

**Ongoing steady state:**

```
Veteran new hires/yr   ≈ 600 (A-14)
Take-up at 10%         = 60 members/yr
Years of credit        = 60 × 2.5 = 150/yr
Gap — nearly all Hybrid, mostly ages 38–50, where the gap is ≈ $0 or negative
Ongoing cost                                                 <  $100,000 / yr
```

**Reported headline: under $2 million one-time, under $100,000 per year ongoing.** The Brief rounds the one-time figure up from $1.46M to "under $2 million" to absorb the HIC impact and modest adverse selection, both excluded from the arithmetic above. **This rounding is a judgment, not a calculation**, and is exactly what VRS actuaries should replace.

### 3.5 Scale comparison

```
Classified payroll  ≈ 56,917 (F-30) × $69,865 (F-34)  ≈  $3.98 billion / yr
One-time cost at the $2.0M ceiling                    =  0.050% of one year's payroll
Annualized over 5 years                               =  0.010% of payroll
```

### 3.6 What a veteran pays today (verified, not modeled)

Using F-19 through F-21 and F-34 ($69,865 mean classified salary), the cost of the full 4-year military purchase **inside** the current window:

| Plan | Rate per year | 1 year | 4 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hybrid | 6.68% | $4,667 | **$18,666** |
| Plan 2 | 10.74% | $7,504 | $30,017 |
| Plan 1 | 12.50% | $8,733 | $34,933 |

Combined with F-27 — payment agreements capped at 12 months and barred from extending past the window — this is the core affordability finding supporting the Brief's DISCUSSION section and the 60-month payment authority in Annex A, § 2.3.
