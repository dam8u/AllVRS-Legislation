# JLC Legislative Change Brief

**Let veterans buy their military service credit at the same price other state employees pay — instead of losing that price after two years on the job.**

## OBJECTIVE

Amend the Virginia Retirement System so that **a veteran working for Virginia who served on active duty before being hired can buy up to four years of military service credit at the standard "normal cost" price at any point in their career** — instead of only during their first 24 months on the job.

Today that price disappears 24 months after a veteran's first day of work. After that the veteran pays a much higher "actuarial" price for the exact same credit.

---

## DISCUSSION

### What veterans can do today

A state employee who served at least 180 consecutive days on active duty, and whose discharge was not dishonorable, may buy up to four years of military service credit toward their VRS pension (§ 51.1-142.2 B 2). That credit is real: it counts toward retiring, toward the size of the pension check, and toward the retiree health insurance credit.

### Where the unfairness is

**The price has an expiration date, and it expires before most veterans can afford it.**

The law says a member pays the "approximate normal cost" — a flat percentage of salary set by the VRS Board. But if the member does not buy, or sign a payment contract, **within the first 24 months of active service following his first date of hire**, the member must instead pay the "actuarial equivalent cost" (§ 51.1-142.2 B 3).

That creates a cliff. Here is what it costs a veteran to buy the full four years at the normal-cost price, using VRS's published rates and the average classified state salary of $69,865:

| Plan the veteran is in | Normal-cost rate, per year of credit | Full 4-year purchase |
|---|---|---|
| Hybrid Retirement Plan (everyone hired since Jan. 1, 2014) | 6.68% of salary | **$18,666** |
| VRS Plan 2 (hired July 2010 – Dec. 2013) | 10.74% of salary | $30,017 |
| VRS Plan 1 (longest-serving employees) | 12.50% of salary | $34,933 |

Three things make that 24-month deadline the wrong deadline:

1. **It lands at the worst possible moment financially.** Veterans come to state service mid-career — the average new state hire is **37.7 years old**. A veteran who just left the military is settling a family, often taking a pay cut, and is at the *bottom* of the state pay scale. Asking for $18,666 in the first two years is asking at the one moment they cannot pay.

2. **Veterans cannot even spread the payments out past the cliff.** VRS payment agreements run 6 to 12 months and, by rule, **may not extend beyond the two-year cost window**. So a veteran cannot simply pay it off slowly. They must find the money fast or lose the price permanently.

3. **Almost nobody knows about it in time.** This is one line buried in a long purchase-of-service statute, explained during onboarding alongside dozens of other forms. By the time a veteran understands what their military service is worth to their pension, the window is shut. The average classified state employee has **10.3 years of service** — five times longer than the window.

The result: a veteran who served four years in the Army and has worked for Virginia for eight years pays dramatically more for the same four years of credit than a brand-new colleague who served the same four years. **Same service to the country, same service credit, different price — and the only difference is how long they have been loyal to the Commonwealth.**

### Who is affected, and how many

| Group | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Classified state employees | 56,917 | DHRM, as of 6/30/2024 |
| Share who are veterans | 6% | DHRM, as of 6/30/2024 |
| **Veterans in the classified state workforce** | **about 3,400** | 6% × 56,917 |
| Of those, how many are already past the 24-month window | roughly 2,900 | average service is 10.3 years |
| New veteran hires each year who would get the fix going forward | roughly 600 | 6% of ~10,300 annual new hires/rehires |

This also directly serves a goal the Commonwealth already set for itself. Virginia gives veterans a hiring preference for state jobs (§ 2.2-2903) and runs the Virginia Values Veterans program to recruit them. It is inconsistent to recruit veterans into state service and then charge them a penalty price on the one benefit that recognizes the service we recruited them for.

### Why this version of the fix, and not a bigger one

This fix is deliberately small. It **does not create any new benefit**. Veterans can already buy this credit, and the credit already counts the way ordinary service credit counts. The bill changes **only the price and the payment schedule** — one sentence. Nothing else in VRS moves: not vesting, not the pension formula, not PLOP, not disability, not survivor benefits. (The full check is in Annex A.)

That matters politically: a bill that changes a price is far easier to pass, and far easier for VRS to administer, than a bill that invents a new kind of service credit.

## COST

### The short answer

**Under $2 million, one time, and under $100,000 a year after that. No general fund appropriation is needed.** The cost is absorbed in the employer contribution rate VRS already certifies.

### Why the cost is small

VRS defines the "actuarial equivalent cost" as *"the amount of money needed in today's dollars to pay for the total value of the increase in the employee's future retirement benefit or earlier retirement eligibility date."* In plain terms: **actuarial cost is the break-even price.** So the entire cost of this bill is the gap between the normal-cost price the veteran pays and that break-even price. Nothing more.

That gap is **much smaller than people assume**, and for younger members it is zero or negative:

| Veteran's age when buying | Hybrid Plan (nearly everyone) | Plan 1 (closed to new hires) |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | veteran **overpays** — the state comes out ahead | plan closed to new hires |
| 50 | about break-even — no real cost | about break-even — no real cost |
| 58 | state absorbs about $1,590 per year of credit | state absorbs about $3,720 per year of credit |

The reason: the Hybrid plan's pension multiplier is only **1.0% of final average pay per year** (§ 51.1-169 D 1). A year of Hybrid credit simply is not worth much, so the normal-cost rate of 6.68% is already close to — and for younger members above — the true cost. **And every veteran hired since January 1, 2014 is in the Hybrid plan.** The expensive-gap cases are limited to the shrinking, closed Plan 1 and Plan 2 populations.

### The arithmetic

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Veterans past the window who could buy, and who meet the 180-day test | ~2,600 |
| Realistic take-up over 5 years (members must pay cash) | 5% – 15% → **130 – 390 members** |
| Average years purchased each | 2.5 of the 4 allowed |
| Years of credit purchased | 325 – 975 |
| Average gap per year of credit (blend of plans and ages) | ~$1,500 |
| **One-time catch-up cost** | **$488,000 – $1,463,000** |
| Spread over five years | roughly $98,000 – $293,000 per year |
| **Ongoing cost after catch-up** (new hires, nearly all Hybrid, gap ≈ 0) | **under $100,000 per year** |

For scale: the Commonwealth's classified payroll is roughly $4.0 billion a year. **The one-time cost is about five one-hundredths of one percent of a single year's payroll.**

> **These figures are JLCVSO analyst estimates, not VRS numbers.** They use VRS's published normal-cost rates, VRS's 6.75% assumed return, the statutory COLA, the 1.0%/1.65%/1.70% multipliers, and DHRM workforce data. Every assumption is listed in Annex B. **VRS actuaries will produce the official number** — under § 30-19.1:7 the VRS Board must file an impact statement on any VRS bill. JLCVSO should ask VRS for that estimate *before* filing.

### The one real fiscal risk, and how the draft handles it

If the price never expires, a member could wait until the eve of retirement and buy credit only when it is obviously profitable — for example, to cross the Rule of 90 or to reach the 15-year threshold for the retiree health insurance credit. That is called adverse selection and it is the objection VRS is most likely to raise.

The draft bill in Annex A answers it directly, with two guardrails that cost veterans nothing:

- **The Board may require any military purchase to be completed at least 12 months before the member's effective date of retirement.** No eve-of-retirement purchases.
- **The Board may set the normal-cost rate for military purchases as a schedule that varies by the member's age.** An older member pays a somewhat higher rate — still a published, predictable, affordable rate, not an individualized actuarial bill.

## RECOMMENDATION

**Amend and reenact § 51.1-142.2 of the Code of Virginia to do three things:**

1. **Remove the 24-month cost deadline for prior active duty military service only.** A veteran may purchase up to four years of military service credit at the approximate normal cost at any time while in service. (Leave the 24-month rule in place for every other category of purchasable service — educational leave, FMLA leave, out-of-state service, and the rest. This keeps the bill narrow and the fiscal note small.)

2. **Let veterans pay over up to 60 months** through payroll deduction, instead of being capped at 12 months inside a closing window. This fixes the cash-flow problem that is the real barrier, and it costs the retirement system nothing.

3. **Give the VRS Board two guardrails** against eve-of-retirement purchases: authority to set an age-graded normal-cost schedule for military purchases, and authority to require that a purchase be completed at least 12 months before retirement.

**No regulatory change is required.** The pricing rule is in statute, so it takes a bill. Once the statute is amended, the VRS Board already has authority to set the applicable normal-cost rate "in its sole discretion," so VRS can implement the rate schedule administratively.

Draft bill language is in **Annex A**, ready for the Division of Legislative Services.

### If the full fix cannot pass

Two fallbacks, in order of preference:

- **Fallback 1 — payment flexibility only (item 2 alone). Zero actuarial cost.** Keep the actuarial price, but let veterans pay it over 60 months at any point in their career. This removes the cash-flow barrier with *no* unfunded liability — only administrative cost. It is extremely hard to oppose on fiscal grounds and makes a good amendment if the price fix draws a negative impact statement.
- **Fallback 2 — a longer window instead of no window.** Extend the military window from 24 to 60 months, and give current employees a one-time 24-month open-enrollment window after the effective date. Caps the exposure while still reaching most veterans.

**Not recommended:** a free "eligibility-only" bridge credit that counts toward retirement eligibility but not the pension amount. It sounds cheaper, but it is not — the veteran pays nothing, so the plan absorbs the whole cost — and it requires inventing a new category of service credit, which multiplies the drafting risk and the chance of unintended spillover. Analysis in Annex A, § 6.

1. **Confirm the scope decision:** state employees only, or also teachers and local government employees? Locals pay their own contribution rates, so including them without an opt-in will draw opposition from the Commission on Local Government. *(Decision needed from JLCVSO — see Annex A, § 7.)*
